Methodology
How estimates are calculated

Your LSAT and GPA are each compared to the school's published 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles from ABA 509 disclosures. Linear interpolation determines where your stats fall within that distribution — your position becomes your "applicant strength" for that school. LSAT is weighted at 60% and GPA at 40%, reflecting typical merit-scholarship practice.

That strength score is then mapped to an estimated grant amount by interpolating across the school's reported full-time grant percentiles (p25 / p50 / p75). If your combined strength puts you near the school's 65th percentile, the estimate sits between that school's p50 and p75 grant amounts.

Confidence reflects how close your stats are to the school's reported enrollment range. "High" means your LSAT and GPA both fall within the school's 25th–75th percentile band. "Low" means one or both fall significantly outside it — the further outside, the less reliable the interpolation.

These are statistical estimates, not guarantees. Actual awards depend on many factors not captured here: application cycle, class composition, demonstrated interest, competing offers, and institutional priorities. Use these figures as a budgeting starting point — real scholarship letters can and do vary significantly from any estimate.

Need-based schools — including Harvard, Yale, and Stanford — award financial aid primarily on demonstrated financial need, not LSAT/GPA credentials. Estimates for those schools are suppressed. Contact each school's financial aid office directly for accurate aid information.
